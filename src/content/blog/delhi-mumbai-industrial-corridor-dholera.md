**Quick answer:** The **Delhi–Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC)** is India's first planned industrial corridor. It grew out of an **India–Japan understanding signed in December 2006**, received **in-principle Cabinet approval in August 2007**, and was given an implementing company, **DMICDC (now NICDC), incorporated 7 January 2008**. Its backbone is the **~1,506 km Western Dedicated Freight Corridor** (Dadri–JNPT), which was **fully completed in September 2026**. Of the corridor's planned industrial cities, **Dholera SIR (920 sq km)** in Gujarat is the largest and the first investment region notified under DMIC.

## 1. Origins: from a 2006 India–Japan idea to a national programme

| Year | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Dec 2006** | India–Japan MoU during the PM's Tokyo visit to cooperate on an industrial corridor along the planned freight line | NICDC; ETH/ISN paper; PIB |
| **Aug 2007** | Union Cabinet gives **in-principle approval** to the DMIC project outline | PIB |
| **7 Jan 2008** | **DMICDC** incorporated as the central SPV | NICDC corporate profile |
| **May 2008** | Dholera approved as a DMIC node | DICDL FAQ |
| **2011** | GoI approves DMIC's financial and institutional structure | NICDC |
| **2015** | CCEA approves trunk infrastructure for Dholera's activation area (₹2,784.83 Cr) and Shendra | NICDC; PIB |
| **Dec 2016** | DMIC Trust renamed **NICDIT**, with a national mandate | NICDC |
| **Feb 2020** | DMICDC renamed **NICDC**. India now plans **11 industrial corridors** nationally | NICDC |
| **Sep 2026** | **Western DFC fully completed** (final 326 km dedicated 8 Sep 2026) | JICA |

**A note on the "GDP crash" story.** Seminars often say DMIC started because India's GDP fell to ~3.1% in 2008. India's growth did slow sharply in FY2008-09 because of the global financial crisis. But DMIC was **conceived in 2006 and approved in 2007**, when growth was high. The more accurate story is that **DMIC was a long-term bet on manufacturing and logistics, made during a boom, and kept going after the crash.**

## 2. Who runs DMIC, and who funds it

- **NICDC** (formerly DMICDC) does project development, master planning and coordination under DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry.
- **NICDIT** (National Industrial Corridor Development & Implementation Trust) channels central funds into city SPVs.
- **City SPVs** such as **DICDL** for Dholera are **51% Gujarat (via DSIRDA, as land)** and **49% Centre (via NICDIT, as cash)** (DICDL).

**NICDC's shareholding (public reporting):** Government of India **49%**, **JBIC (Japan) 26%**, HUDCO 19.9%, IIFCL 4.1%, LIC 1%.

> **How this squares with the deck's "74% India / 26% Japan":** Add up the Indian public shareholders (GoI 49% + HUDCO 19.9% + IIFCL 4.1% + LIC 1%) and you get **74%**. Japan's JBIC holds **26%**. So the 74:26 split is right as **India-side vs Japan-side**, but the 74% is not all held "via DMIC Trust".

**Japan's wider role:** Separately, JICA lent about **¥620.8 billion (~₹37,658 Cr)** to build the Western DFC (JICA, Sep 2026).

**Project size:** Total DMIC investment has been estimated at **around US$90–100 billion** (PIB has used "around US$100 billion"). This is an overall programme estimate over many years, not one budget.

## 3. Geography: 6 states, a 150–200 km influence band

DMIC runs through **Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and Maharashtra**, along a band roughly **150 km on either side** of the freight corridor (official concept; the deck says 150–200 km).

### The planned nodes
DMIC's perspective plan identified **24 nodes: 11 Investment Regions (≥200 sq km) and 13 Industrial Areas (≥100 sq km)**. The deck's "11 industrial nodes" refers to the 11 Investment Regions.

**Priority cities being built under NICDC (examples):**
| Node | State | Notes |

## 4. The four transport pillars, and their real status

### 🚆 Rail: Western Dedicated Freight Corridor (WDFC)
- **Route:** Dadri (UP) → JNPT (Mumbai), **~1,506 km** (JICA; deck says 1,504 km, NICDC says 1,504 km).
- **Status:** **Fully completed and operational.** The final three sections (326 km, ₹20,703 Cr) were dedicated on **8 Sep 2026** (JICA). *The deck says "operational 31 March 2026"; that is when the last stretches were nearing completion and trials were reported. Full dedication came in September 2026.*
- **Why it matters:** Fast, high-capacity freight trains to Gujarat and Maharashtra ports cut logistics costs, and logistics cost is what makes a manufacturing location competitive.

### 🛣️ Road: Delhi–Mumbai Expressway (DME)
- **Length:** **1,386 km** main alignment; **1,445 km** including spurs (MoRTH reply in Parliament, Mar 2026). *The deck says 1,480 km.*
- **Status:** **929 km operational** as of March 2026 (about 64%). The Delhi–Vadodara section was targeted for mid-2026; remaining Gujarat packages were still under construction as of that Lok Sabha reply (Nitin Gadkari, via News18). *The deck's "1,169 km (~78%) operational, Delhi to Navsari" may be a later or differently counted figure. We use the latest official parliamentary number and suggest re-checking before publishing.*
- **Design features (per NHAI/deck):** 8 lanes expandable, wayside amenities, wildlife crossings and water harvesting. Travel-time target is Delhi–Mumbai in **~12 hours** (down from ~24).

### ✈️ Air: Dholera International Airport
- CCEA approved Phase-1 on **14 Jun 2022** (₹1,305 Cr; DIACL = AAI 51 : Gujarat 33 : NICDIT 16).
- First calibration aircraft landed in **June 2026**. The runway, taxiway and ATC are complete, and operations are targeted for **late 2026**, subject to DGCA licensing.
- Site area of ~1,426 ha (CAPA); Phase-1 runway of **3,200 m** (CAPA). *The deck's 4,000 m runway matches the long-term master plan's second runway, not Phase-1.*

### ⚓ Sea: Gujarat's port advantage
Gujarat is India's leading maritime state, handling about **40% of India's port cargo** (deck; widely cited). Port distances from Dholera (deck figures, road distance approximate): **Ghogha ~74 km · Pipavav ~200 km · Kandla ~300 km · Mundra ~350 km**, plus access to Dahej and Hazira. The **UAE LoI (19 Jan 2026)** also includes a **greenfield port** for Dholera (PIB).

## 5. Why Dholera is DMIC's flagship node

| Factor | Dholera SIR |
|---|---|
| **Size** | **920 sq km**, larger than Singapore (~728–735 sq km). About 422 sq km is under town-planning schemes TP1–TP6 |
| **Legal base** | Gujarat SIR Act 2009 (in force 6 Jan 2009). DSIR notified May 2009. DSIRDA formed 16 Feb 2010 |
| **Delivery vehicle** | DICDL (incorporated 28 Jan 2016), 51% Gujarat / 49% Centre |
| **Built today** | 22.54 sq km activation area: trunk roads, water treatment, CETP, stormwater, ICT, ABCD building |
| **Road** | **Ahmedabad–Dholera Expressway (NE-8)**, 109 km, 4-lane expandable to 8, inaugurated **31 Mar 2026**; Ahmedabad–Dholera **83 km / ~45 min** (PIB) |
| **Rail** | **Sarkhej–Dholera semi-high-speed line** approved **13 May 2026**: 134 km including spurs, 200 km/h, ₹20,667 Cr (PIB) |
| **Air** | Dholera International Airport, near-ready (see above) |
| **Sea** | Gulf of Khambhat coastline; Ghogha nearby; UAE LoI for a greenfield port |
| **Anchor industry** | Tata–PSMC fab (₹91,000 Cr); solar; data-centre policy priority hub |

**The deck's claim "largest SIR with all four transport modes":** This is a reasonable description of Dholera's *planned* connectivity. Today **road is fully operational, air is close, rail is approved but not built, and the port is at LoI stage**, so "all four" is a target, not yet a fact.

## 6. What DMIC means for land investors (no-hype version)

**Positive signals**
- The corridor's two backbones (WDFC fully, DME partly) are physically built, so this is no longer only on paper.
- Dholera has central funding at every layer: trunk infrastructure, airport, rail and fab incentives.
- Gujarat's 2026–29 Data Centre Policy adds a new demand driver.

**Risks to weigh**
- Industrial corridors take **decades**. Dholera is planned in phases over many years.
- Infrastructure lifts land unevenly. Plots near the activation area, the expressway interchanges and planned stations benefit first.
- Liquidity is thin. Plan for a long hold and check exit options.
- Legal diligence is non-negotiable: NA order, title chain, TP-scheme status and CRZ applicability.

## FAQ

**What is DMIC?**
The Delhi–Mumbai Industrial Corridor is a government-led programme to build planned industrial cities along the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor across six states. It is coordinated by NICDC.

**When did DMIC start?**
An India–Japan MoU was signed in December 2006. The Cabinet gave in-principle approval in August 2007, and DMICDC was incorporated on 7 January 2008.

**Is Japan funding DMIC?**
Japan's JBIC holds 26% of NICDC. Separately, JICA has lent about ₹37,658 crore for the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor.

**How many nodes does DMIC have?**
24 planned nodes (11 Investment Regions and 13 Industrial Areas). Phase-1 priority cities include Dholera, Shendra–Bidkin, Vikram Udyogpuri and Greater Noida.

**Is the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor complete?**
Yes. The final sections were dedicated on 8 September 2026, completing the ~1,506 km Dadri–JNPT corridor.

**How far is Dholera from Ahmedabad now?**
About 83 km and ~45 minutes via the Ahmedabad–Dholera Expressway (PIB, Feb 2026).

**Is Dholera part of the Delhi–Mumbai Expressway?**
No. Dholera connects through the separate Ahmedabad–Dholera Expressway (NE-8) and Gujarat's wider road network.
